The 1968 Genesis

In a landmark 1968 article published in Science, Dr. Peter Glaser introduced the concept of transmitting solar energy from space to Earth. He recognized that terrestrial solar is inherently limited by the atmosphere, weather, and the day/night cycle.

Glaser proposed placing enormous solar collector arrays in Geostationary Earth Orbit (GEO). In this orbit, satellites face the sun almost continuously, gathering intense, unfiltered solar radiation, converting it to microwaves, and beaming it down to massive terrestrial receiving antennas (rectennas) for continuous baseload power.

Japan's OHISAMA Project

Moving from theory to practice, Japan is aggressively pursuing SBSP. Following successful ground tests of microwave power transmission in 2015, the Japanese government and JAXA aim to conduct an orbital test by 2025.

The test will utilize small satellites in Low Earth Orbit (LEO) at an altitude of approximately 400 to 450 kilometers. This crucial experiment will attempt to beam kilowatts of power to terrestrial receiving stations.

Target Milestones:

  • 2025: LEO Orbital Transmission Test (450km)
  • 2030s: First large-scale commercial orbital plant
  • Goal: Provide gigawatt-scale power to resource-scarce nations.
